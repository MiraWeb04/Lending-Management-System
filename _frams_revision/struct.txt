[548] Formula 8. Final Due Date
[549] FD = RD + n months
[551] FD represents the final due date, RD is the loan release date, and n is the term in months. The final due-date rule may cause the final interval to differ from the regular interval in some schedules.
[552] Financial Risk Assessment and Eligibility
[553] The financial risk function produces an eligibility estimate for reviewer use. It is not an approval and does not predict default. The function reads monthly gross income or an alternative monthly income field when gross income is missing, existing debt payments, other monthly obligations, employment status, employment or business duration, requested loan amount, loan term, and interest rate. Dependents and collateral notes are stored with the application but are not variables in the numerical f
[555] Policy values are supplied through the environment or code defaults. The defaults described for the system are a maximum DTI of 40%, minimum loan amount of ₱3,000, maximum loan amount of ₱500,000, minimum employment or business duration of three months, and a standard monthly interest rate of 5%.
[557] Formula 9. Existing Debt-to-Income Ratio
[558] DTI_before = (E / I) × 100
[560] E is existing monthly debt payments plus other monthly obligations, and I am monthly gross income.
[562] Formula 10. Proposed Debt-to-Income Ratio
[563] DTI_after = (E + MP) / I) × 100
[565] MP is the proposed monthly payment. The system description specifies that this payment is calculated as total payable divided by the loan term in months.
[567] Formula 11. Proposed Monthly Payment
[568] MP = TP / n
[570] The proposed monthly payment is used for the monthly DTI comparison. It should not be confused with an individual weekly, daily, or semi-monthly installment.
[572] Formula 12. Maximum Affordable New Payment
[573] MAP = (I × (Dmax / 100)) − E
[575] MAP is the maximum affordable new monthly payment, Dmax is the maximum DTI percentage (default 40%), I is monthly gross income, and E is existing monthly obligations.
[577] Formula 13. Maximum Affordable Principal
[578] Pmax = (MAP × n) / [1 + ((r / 100) × n)]
[580] Pmax is the maximum affordable principal before applying the configured loan limits, n is the term in months, and r is the monthly interest rate expressed as a percentage. The result is limited by the configured minimum and maximum loan amounts: minimum ₱3,000 and maximum ₱500,000 by default.

It assigns Conditionally Eligible when the requested amount exceeds the maximum affordable amount or other policy reasons remain. It assigns Eligible when the affordability figures fall within the limits and no disqualifying policy reason remains. [583] Payment Processing, Remaining Balance, and Penalties
[584] The system validates payments before recording them. The payment must be greater than zero. A penalty cannot exceed the amount received. The amount received cannot exceed the remaining scheduled loan balance plus the penalty still due, and the installment portion cannot exceed the remaining scheduled balance.
[586] Formula 14. Cumulative Amount Paid
[587] AP = Σ Aᵢ, for i = 1 to m
[589] AP is the sum of amounts recorded as paid toward the loan, Aᵢ is the amount for transaction i, and m is the number of payment transactions included in the sum.
[591] Formula 15. Remaining Loan Balance
[592] RB = TP − AP
[594] RB is the remaining scheduled loan balance, TP is the total scheduled principal-and-interest payable, and AP is the amount applied to that scheduled payable. Penalties are tracked separately and are excluded from RB. For example, if TP is ₱13,000 and the amount applied to the scheduled payable is ₱5,000, the remaining scheduled balance is ₱8,000. Any penalty included in a payment must not also be counted as an amount applied to the scheduled balance.
[596] An installment is overdue when its due date has passed and it remains unpaid. The described implementation calculates a penalty for an overdue unpaid installment by multiplying the installment total by a rate associated with the payment frequency.
