[27] Background of Study
[28] Many lending offices maintain borrower files, loan computations, and payment records using paper-based documents or separate spreadsheets. This practice makes it difficult to access and monitor essential information in one place, including borrower applications, declared income, existing financial obligations, loan amounts released, and outstanding balances. When these records are not properly integrated, lending offices may experience delays in processing transactions, inconsistencies in financial records, and difficulties monitoring overdue accounts and repayment activities.
[29] Credit risk in micro-lending involves more than determining whether an individual qualifies for a loan. Local studies of lending institutions indicate that lending officers continue to rely on borrower interviews, business verification, and checks for existing unpaid loans when evaluating applications. These practices are particularly relevant because many micro-borrowers have limited income and little formal credit history (Mina, 2024; Juventud et al., 2023). Furthermore, studies on digital lending and microfinance indicate the growing role of web-based systems and structured risk assessment tools in improving lending operations (Santos, 2023; Campos, 2024). These studies, discussed in detail in Chapter 2, support the development of an integrated system that connects borrower information, loan processing, and financial risk assessment.
